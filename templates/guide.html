Setup guide

The same guide is built into the app: open it and click ? Guide.

Everything you need to get {{ app_name }} running on your own PC, from a fresh Windows install to your first story. No programming needed: each step says exactly where to click.

How it fits together

Three pieces, all on your own PC:

PieceWhat it does
LM StudioRuns the AI model on your graphics card. It's the "brain", and works without the internet once the model is downloaded.
{{ app_name }}This app. It keeps your worlds, characters and stories, builds what the model reads, and shows the chat in your browser.
Your browserWhere you chat, on this PC at {{ pc_url }}, or on your phone over your home Wi-Fi.

Nothing is sent anywhere else. Your stories are plain text files in a folder on your PC.

What you need

  • A Windows PC with an NVIDIA graphics card, ideally with 8 GB of video memory or more (e.g. RTX 3060, 4060, 4070). Other cards work in LM Studio too, just slower.
  • About 15 GB of free disk space: most of it for the model.
  • About 30 minutes for the first setup, mostly waiting for downloads.

1Install Python

The app is written in Python, so Windows needs it installed once.

  1. Go to python.org → Downloads and download the latest Python for Windows (3.11 or newer).
  2. Run the installer. On the first screen, tick "Add python.exe to PATH" at the bottom. This matters: without it, Windows can't find Python later.
  3. Click Install Now and wait until it says it's done.
Check Press Win, type cmd, press Enter. In the black window type python --version and press Enter. You should see something like Python 3.12.4.

2Set up LM Studio

Install it and download a model

  1. Download LM Studio from lmstudio.ai and install it.
  2. Click the 🔍 magnifying glass in its left sidebar and search for a model. Two good starting points for a card with 12 GB:
    • Qwen3.5 9B: fast, good all-rounder.
    • MN-12B-Mag-Mell-R1 (12B, search Mag-Mell): our pick for roleplay. Natural prose, follows your story, open to mature content.
    • Mistral Nemo Instruct 2407 (12B): steady and reliable, keeps things tame.
    With 8 GB, stay around 7–9B. See Choosing a model Testing a new model.
  3. In the download options on the right, pick Q4_K_M (a good balance of size and quality) and click Download.

Set how much it can remember

  1. Click My Models (the folder icon on the left), then the ⚙ gear next to your model.
  2. Set Context Length to 16384. If LM Studio warns that the model no longer fits fully on the GPU, use 12288 or 8192.

Let it run in the background

  1. Open LM Studio's settings: Ctrl + ,
  2. Click Developer on the left.
  3. Turn on Enable Local LLM Service (under "Local LLM Service (headless)"). The model server now runs whenever you're logged in, even with the LM Studio window closed.
  4. On the same page, keep Just-in-time model loading on. LM Studio then loads the model by itself when the first message arrives, and unloads it after a while without use (Max idle TTL) to free your graphics card.
Check Open http://localhost:1234/v1/models in your browser. A page of text listing your model means LM Studio is ready.

3Install the app

  1. Unzip the {{ app_name }} zip (the one you were sent, or from GitHub: Code → Download ZIP) somewhere permanent, e.g. Documents\{{ app_name }}. Not in Downloads, since you'll keep using this folder. To unzip: right-click the zip → Extract All…
  2. Open that folder in File Explorer. Click the address bar at the top, type cmd and press Enter. A black window opens, already in the right folder.
  3. Type this and press Enter: 
    pip install -r requirements.txt
    Wait until it finishes (a minute or two), then close the black window. You only do this once, and again after an update that adds new add-ons.

4Start the app

  1. Double-click start.bat in the app's folder.
  2. After a few seconds your browser opens the app, and a small blue speech-bubble icon appears near the clock. If you don't see it, click the ^ arrow next to the clock.
  3. Right-click the icon and tick Start with Windows. From now on the app is there whenever your PC is on.
  4. Bookmark {{ pc_url }}.

Your first world

A world holds everything for one setting: its rules and places, its characters, side characters, and who you play as. Characters in one world can only meet players from that world.

On the start screen, click + New world. The editor has two groups of steps:

World

  • The basics: name, genre, tone, era.
  • World foundations: what every character always knows. The World overview, World rules (magic, technology, what's impossible), Peoples & Species, Politics & Current Affairs, and the Writing format every reply follows. These are sent with every message, so keep them to the essentials.
  • Places & Lore: details that are only added when their name comes up in the chat: towns, factions, history, objects. Add as many as you like. Tick Always on only for something every character must always know.

People

  • Characters: who you roleplay with. Their sheet has four parts: Who they are (name, age, appearance, personality, flaws), Inner life (motives and beliefs, love and relationships, backstory), In the world (skills, daily life, people, likes and dislikes, key memories) and Voice (how they speak). Only name and personality are needed; the rest open with + Add. Plus one or more story premises (ways a story with them can begin).
  • NPCs: side characters of the world who join a scene when they're mentioned (you don't chat with them directly), with their own memories and status (an NPC who dies stays dead in that story).
  • Players: who you play as. Public is what anyone would know about you; Private is never sent to the model.
Tip Every field has an ⓘ with an explanation, and empty fields show a greyed-out example. After the first setup you can edit characters and players straight from the start screen (✎ Edit) without going through the steps.

Starting a story

  1. On the start screen, pick the world, then under a character choose who you Play as.
  2. Click Start new story. Set the scene opens: pick one of the character's premises, tweak the text if you like, and choose whether the character speaks first or you open the scene.
  3. Click Start story. Next time, the same button says Continue.

By default a character has never met your player: they don't know your name until you tell them, unless the premise says you know each other.

In the chat

  • Send with Enter, new line with Shift + Enter. Write actions in *asterisks*.
  • ■ Stop stops a reply mid-way. Reroll under the last reply writes another version; flip between versions with ‹ ›.
  • Hover a message (tap on a phone) to edit or delete it.
  • Let someone speak: the chips above the message box let the character continue, or an NPC jump in. Type @ to address an NPC directly.
  • The pill in the message box sets the story's flair (Roleplay, Storyteller, Companion, Casual, or None).
  • Journal (top right) shows the current location, what the character has learned about you, where you stand, key events, and each NPC's status. It updates itself as you play, and you can edit it. The location also shows under the character's name (📍), and that place's Places & Lore entry stays loaded while you're there, so write region first (e.g. "Aestria, the road to Houlton"). You can set where a story begins in Set the scene.
  • Aa changes text size, font and colour theme (15 themes, dark and light; also under ◐ Theme on the start screen, saved per device). A chat background picture can be added per character (in their editor, under the portrait) or for a whole world (under The basics); Aa lets you dim it or turn it off. ⇔ changes the chat width. ⇄ (top left) switches story.
  • The dot next to the model name: ready, model will load on your next message (takes a few seconds), LM Studio can't be reached.

Long chats don't get lost: older messages are folded into short summaries the character keeps, shown as a line in the chat.

Better replies

Small local models copy examples far more than they follow descriptions. In order of impact:

  1. Example message (character page): write one reply exactly the way you want replies to look, in length, layout and tone. This is the strongest lever.
  2. Writing format (World foundations): the layout rules for everyone in the world, e.g. "Third person, present tense. Actions in *asterisks*, speech on its own line. Keep replies under 750 characters."
  3. Response directive (character page): their voice in a few words. It's read before every reply, so keep it short and leave out anything situational.
  4. Personality: describe what they do ("teases with a straight face") rather than adjectives ("smug, dramatic"), which small models exaggerate.
  5. Creativity around 0.8–0.9 and flair None if replies feel over-the-top.

After changing a character, start a new story to test: an existing chat's history also shapes the style.

Choosing a model

The model decides how well characters write, how much they remember and what they're willing to write. The app works with any chat model in LM Studio, and switching is one click in the chat header.

How to pick one

  • Size: pick a model whose download (in Q4_K_M) is a few GB smaller than your video memory, so the conversation fits on the card too.
  • Roleplay versions (fine-tunes) are trained on fiction and usually write more naturally than the plain "Instruct" models. Pick ones with many downloads, and read their page in LM Studio.
  • Who uploaded it matters. The same model is often uploaded by several people. Prefer the model's makers, lmstudio-community, bartowski or mradermacher. For mradermacher, the i1 versions are slightly better at the same size.
  • Skip models with "Thinking" in the name for roleplay: they reason before every reply, which mostly adds waiting.

Testing a new model

  1. Test it in LM Studio first. In LM Studio's own Chat tab, load the model and say "Hello". You should get a normal sentence. If LM Studio's chat already gives nonsense, the problem is the model or LM Studio, not the app, so don't continue until this works.
  2. Set its Context Length (My Models → ⚙) before it's first used, then Eject it so the next load uses the new setting. A newly downloaded model starts with a small default.
  3. Pick it in the app from the model menu in the chat header and click Model Settings → Test model for a quick check through the app.
  4. Compare fairly: start a new story with the same character and use Reroll a few times. Existing chats carry the old model's style.
  5. Open the model menu: if it says Shorter memory, the model's Context Length is on the small side. It still works, the app fits itself to it, but raising it in LM Studio lets the character remember more.
If a model writes gibberish

Symbols, dots or a jumble of languages (in LM Studio's chat as well) mean that copy of the model doesn't run on your setup. In order:

  1. Reset its load settings: My Models → ⚙, keep K/V cache quantization off, then Eject and try again.
  2. Try another upload of the same model (another uploader in the search results).
  3. Try another version of the model (e.g. v1.2 instead of v1.3): sometimes a release itself is faulty.
  4. Update LM Studio's engine: Settings → Runtime (Ctrl + Shift + R), update the llama.cpp engines, or switch between CUDA and Vulkan.
  5. Still nothing: pick a model of another family (e.g. a Llama based one instead of Mistral).

The app refuses to save replies that are just symbols, and keeps the last request and the model's raw answer in logs\last-reply.txt for troubleshooting.

If replies look broken in other ways Replies that start with the character's name, contain odd tags like <|im_start|>, or never stop usually mean a prompt format mismatch. Check Model Settings → Prompt format is on Auto, or try Model's own template, then Test model.

On your phone

  1. On the PC: ⚙ Model Settings → Phone & Other Devices. Tick Allow devices on my home network, set a passphrase and click Save access.
  2. Right-click the tray icon → Restart. The first time, Windows asks whether to allow Python on networks: tick Private networks and click Allow.
  3. On your phone, on the same Wi-Fi, open the address shown in Model Settings (like http://192.168.1.23:5000) and enter the passphrase once.
  4. To make it feel like an app: Safari Share → Add to Home Screen (Android: ⋮ → Add to Home screen).

It works while the PC is on. The PC itself never asks for the passphrase; changing it logs other devices out.

Home network only. Never make {{ app_name }} reachable from the internet (no port forwarding, no "expose to the web" tools): the passphrase is basic protection and the connection isn't encrypted.

Updating the app

  1. Copy the new files over the old ones in the app's folder (Windows: Replace the files in the destination). Your worlds are in the worlds folder, which updates never include, so nothing of yours is touched.
  2. Then, depending on what changed:
    • only files in templates or static: refresh the page (F5);
    • app.py: tray icon → Restart, then refresh;
    • tray.pyw: tray icon → Quit, then start.bat;
    • requirements.txt: run the pip install line from step 3 again first.

If a page says the app needs a restart, that's the sign the page was updated but the app wasn't restarted yet.

Your files & backups

  • Everything lives in the worlds folder inside the app's folder: one folder per world, with readable .md text files you can also edit in Notepad or Obsidian.
  • A backup zip is made once a day in backups; the last 7 are kept. To restore, quit the app and unzip one over the worlds folder.
  • Deleting anything in the app moves it to worlds\.trash, so it can be recovered.
  • Export / Import on a character's or player's page saves them as one .json file (with portrait and premises, never your stories), to keep a copy or share with a friend.
  • Advanced settings (port, address, folder location) are in config.toml; restart after changing it.

Responsible use

  • Adults only for mature content. Mature content is off by default. When it's on, every reply carries the rule that everyone involved is a consenting adult. A character whose Age is under 18 (or describes them as a child or teenager) is never part of romantic or mature content, whatever the settings; this also applies to imported characters. Players and NPCs have an Age too: if the character or the player is under 18, the whole story stays free of romance and mature content, and an NPC under 18 is never part of it.
  • Check each model's licence. What a model may be used for is set by its makers, not by {{ app_name }}. Some licences forbid mature content outright (Google's Gemma models, for example), so read the model's page before using it for that.
  • Keep it on your home network. See On your phone: never expose the app to the internet.
  • Your stories are plain files. Chats, Journals and backups are ordinary text files in the worlds folder. Anyone with access to the PC can read them.
  • Sharing characters. Exported characters contain their description, premises and pictures, never your stories. Only share what you'd be happy for others to see, and check characters you import from others.
